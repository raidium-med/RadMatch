## TASK

For each matched pair of findings (pred vs gt), list the attribute errors: how the pred finding's description differs from the gt finding's. Judge only the dimensions of §2. `clinical_status` (normal vs abnormal), the side (`laterality`: left, right, bilateral), comparison labels, and counts, ratios and attenuation are scored deterministically upstream: do not repeat them. Sizes and changes over time are flagged upstream; you grade the ones that matter (§2 `measurement` and `comparison`).

Input: a JSON object with `series_uuid` (context only), an optional study `indication`, and `pairs`: a list of `{"pair_id": <int>, "pred_finding": {...}, "gt_finding": {...}}`, numbered from 1. Each finding has the standard fields (`text`, `clinical_status`, `clinical_significance`, etc.). When one finding was matched to several on the other side, the pair also carries `same_group_pred` / `same_group_gt`: the texts of the other findings in its match group.

---

## 1. DECISION RULES (apply to every pair)

1. **Status inversion → no errors.** If one finding is normal and the other abnormal, return `"errors": []` for that pair: the inversion is scored upstream. **Two normal findings → no errors**, however differently they are worded or whatever structures or stations one names and the other leaves out ("no mediastinal or axillary lymphadenopathy" vs "no enlarged lymph nodes"): they differ only by template. The one exception is a hedge on what is ruled out (`certainty`: "possibly no pulmonary embolism" vs "no pulmonary embolism"). **Two routine findings → minor at most**: when both findings are `routine` (benign or low-impact, no follow-up), no difference between them changes management, so none is `major`.
2. **Judge the pair against its group.** Report errors of the pair's own two findings under its own `pair_id`. Within a group, a gt detail that another pred finding of the group states is not missing, and pred content that describes another gt finding of the group is not an error for this pair ("bilateral hydronephrosis" vs a left and a right hydronephrosis: no error on either pair). An umbrella pred that does not state this gt finding's own site, level or size has left it out (rule 3): pred "enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes" vs gt "12 mm right paratracheal node" → `site` major (the station sets the stage and the sampling; "enlarged" covers the size).
3. **Leaving a detail out is a minor error unless the detail changes management; adding one is not an error.** A detail the gt gives that the pred leaves out (lobe, segment, level, site, extent, severity grade, character, diagnosis, subtype, size or age; the side is scored upstream) is an error of that dimension, graded `minor`: the pred is partly right, not wrong (pred "atelectasis" vs gt "right middle lobe atelectasis" → `site` minor; pred "splenomegaly" vs gt "mild splenomegaly" → `severity` minor). It is `major` only when the missing detail would change management, the cases each dimension of §2 lists: an actionable diagnosis or an acute age, a site that guides staging, a procedure or a follow-up, a feature that signals a complication, malignancy or urgency, a structured category, a high grade, the size of a lesion followed or staged by its size, invasion, mass effect or a complication. A pred more detailed than the gt is not an error (pred "consolidation in the posterior basal segment of the right lower lobe" vs gt "right lower lobe consolidation" → `[]`), except a clinical claim the gt does not support: an added complication, extension or actionable diagnosis is an error (§2 `extent`, `diagnosis`). A detail the exam already implies is not missing ("aortic calcification" on an abdominal CT). A pair where neither side gives anything beyond the bare anatomy + pathology → `[]`.
4. **Grade each error** with the `severity` field. `major` means a radiologist would manage the patient differently; `minor` means the pred is partly right, or differs without consequence:
   - `"major"`: the two descriptions are mutually exclusive AND the difference changes management; a left-out gt detail that changes management (rule 3).
   - `"minor"`: both findings give the detail and differ without changing management, so a clinician would not be misled (mild vs moderate); a left-out gt detail that does not change management (rule 3); the minor cases each dimension lists.
5. **Do not invent differences, and do not drop left-out details.** When the two findings give the same detail in different words, do not emit. A left-out gt detail is still an error: report it, as `minor` when it changes nothing (rule 3); it is not a case of doubt.
6. **One difference, one dimension.** Report each difference once, under the dimension whose definition it fits; the boundaries are given in §2.

---

## 2. DIMENSIONS

Use only these eight `dimension` names.

**`site`**: where the finding sits within the side: lobe, segment, zone, level, quadrant, station, organ part, vessel, device tip position. The side itself (left, right, bilateral) is scored upstream from each finding's `laterality`: never report a side difference, and judge two findings on different sides by their site alone (pred "right lower lobe" vs gt "left lower lobe" → `[]`; pred "right middle lobe" vs gt "left lower lobe" → a different lobe). How far the finding reaches is `extent`.
- major: a focal lesion that needs follow-up, workup or treatment (nodule, mass, lymph node, fracture, collection, stone) placed in a different lobe, segment, level, quadrant, station, organ part or vessel; a device tip in a different structure (pred "lower SVC" vs gt "upper right atrium"); a second location the pred adds for such a lesion that neither the gt nor the group has; a gt site the pred leaves out when it guides staging, a procedure or the follow-up of a lesion that needs one: a lymph node station, the lobe or segment of a mass or of a nodule at or above a follow-up threshold, the organ part or segment of a lesion that needs workup or resection (pred "colonic mass" vs gt "sigmoid colon mass"), the vertebral level of a spinal fracture or lesion, the bone of a fracture (not the number of a rib), a device tip position.
- minor: a gt site the pred leaves out otherwise: a diffuse process, a benign or routine finding, a small nodule that is not followed, an expected post-treatment state (pred "atelectasis" vs gt "right middle lobe atelectasis"; pred "status post partial pancreatectomy" vs gt "pancreatic body and tail absent after surgery"); two words for the same site that a reader could not tell apart ("left base" vs "left lower lobe"); a coarser or partly different distribution of a diffuse or multifocal process (ground glass, consolidation pattern, atelectasis, emphysema, steatosis: "bilateral ground glass in the upper and lower lobes" vs "bilateral subpleural ground glass"); a different site of a finding that needs no follow-up.
- no error: the same site; a difference of side only; a pred more precise than the gt (a segment of the gt lobe; the lingula for gt "left upper lobe"); a clearly midline structure (aorta, IVC, SVC, heart, esophagus, trachea, bladder, prostate, uterus, vertebrae, rectum) with no sub-anatomic detail differing.

**`extent`**: how far and how many: one lesion vs several, focal vs multifocal vs diffuse, distribution and predominance, extension or invasion into a neighbouring structure, vascular involvement, mass effect, and complications (perforation, obstruction, abscess, rupture, herniation). A count given as a number is `measurement`; a grade word (mild, large) is `severity`.
- major: one lesion vs several, for lesions that need follow-up or treatment (a solitary vs multiple metastases or nodules); confined vs extending or invading (abutment vs invasion of the mediastinum, abutment vs encasement of a vessel); extension to another organ; mass effect or a complication that only one of the two findings gives, also when the pred adds it (pred "closed-loop obstruction with a transition point" vs gt "small bowel obstruction").
- minor: a different predominant site or distribution of the same process ("sigmoid-predominant" vs "descending-colon-predominant" diverticulosis); how far a process spreads, given by the gt and left out by the pred, when it changes nothing (pred "ascites" vs gt "ascites extending into the pelvis").
- no error: the same extent in different words; a difference of side only (one side vs both is scored upstream).

**`severity`**: qualitative magnitude: mild / moderate / severe, small / large, percentage effacement, and structured categories.
- major: a change of structured category that changes management, even one step apart (Bosniak II vs IIF, Lung-RADS 3 vs 4A, BI-RADS, LI-RADS); a change that crosses a clinical-action threshold: grades two steps apart (minimal or mild ↔ marked or severe, trace ↔ large); a structured category the gt gives that the pred leaves out, when it calls for follow-up or treatment (pred "left renal cyst" vs gt "Bosniak IIF left renal cyst"; a left-out Bosniak II is minor); a high grade the gt gives that the pred leaves out (severe, marked, massive, large, critical: pred "splenomegaly" vs gt "marked splenomegaly"; pred "right pneumothorax" vs gt "large right pneumothorax").
- minor: one step apart (mild ↔ moderate, small ↔ moderate, moderate ↔ large); any difference of hepatic steatosis grade (CT grading is imprecise and does not drive management); a low or middle grade the gt gives that the pred leaves out (mild, minimal, trace, small, slight, moderate: pred "spleen is enlarged" vs gt "spleen is mildly enlarged").
- no error: neither side uses a descriptor, both use the same one or near-synonyms, or only the pred gives one.

**`character`**: what the finding looks like: shape, margin, composition, density or signal, enhancement, calcification, fat, gas, cavitation, septa, air-fluid levels. What the finding is called is `diagnosis`.
- major: spiculated vs smooth on a mass; a composition or feature that changes the differential (benign-pattern calcification vs none, solid vs ground-glass, cystic vs solid, fat vs fluid, pleural fluid vs pleural thickening, enhancing vs non-enhancing); spiculation, cavitation or macroscopic fat that the pred adds to a nodule the gt describes without it; a feature that signals a complication, malignancy or urgency and that only one of the two findings gives (gas in a collection or an effusion, necrosis, cavitation, spiculation, an enhancing solid component, air-fluid levels in dilated bowel the pred does not call obstructed).
- minor: two words for nearly the same appearance ("ill-defined" vs "hazy"); different descriptors of one diffuse process (ground glass with or without consolidation or septal thickening, described by two readers); a descriptive feature the gt gives that the pred leaves out when it signals no complication, malignancy or urgency, or when the pred already names what it signals (air-fluid levels left out by a pred that calls an obstruction; pred "lung nodules" vs gt "partially calcified lung nodules"; pred "hypodense pancreatic tail lesion" vs gt "cystic pancreatic tail lesion").
- no error: neither side describes the appearance, or only the pred does without changing the differential.

**`diagnosis`**: what the abnormality is: the named entity, its subtype, its age (acute, subacute, chronic, healed), the cause of a secondary finding, the device type. The appearance is `character`, the hedge `certainty`, structured categories (Bosniak, Lung-RADS) `severity`; a change since a prior study is `comparison` ("new" is not "acute").
- major: two committed diagnoses that exclude each other and change management for the same abnormality (bulla vs pneumothorax, atelectasis vs pneumonia, diverticulosis vs diverticulitis, a normal variant vs a neoplasm, reactive vs malignant nodes, cyst vs solid tumor); a cause that changes management (pred "duct dilatation after cholecystectomy" vs gt "duct dilatation from a pancreatic head mass"); a subtype that changes management (type A vs type B dissection); acute vs chronic or healed (acute vs chronic emboli, acute vs healed fracture), and an "acute" the gt gives that the pred leaves out on a critical or urgent finding; an actionable gt diagnosis (pneumonia, abscess, malignancy, appendicitis, dissection, embolism) that the pred gives only as an appearance or a generic term (pred "opacity" vs gt "pneumonia"); an actionable diagnosis the pred adds that the gt does not support (pred "hepatic metastasis" vs gt "indeterminate hepatic lesion").
- minor: a generic term or an appearance for a gt diagnosis or subtype that is not actionable (pred "hypodense lesion" vs gt "simple cyst"; pred "emphysema" vs gt "centrilobular emphysema"); two names on one spectrum or with the same management ("infiltrate" vs "consolidation"); a different device type (PICC vs port); neighbouring ages (subacute vs acute, subacute vs chronic).
- no error: synonyms; the pred names the diagnosis of what the gt describes (pred "bronchiectasis" vs gt "dilated bronchi"); a pred differential that contains the gt diagnosis (a hedge: `certainty`); an age only the pred gives.

**`certainty`**: diagnostic certainty / hedging: definite vs probable vs possible vs cannot-rule-out.
- Ladder: consistent with / diagnostic of > suspicious for / probably > possibly / may represent / versus artifact > less likely > unlikely.
- major: two or more steps apart: gt "enlarged pulmonary artery, may reflect pulmonary hypertension" vs pred "diagnostic of pulmonary hypertension"; pred "possible rib fracture" vs gt "rib fracture".
- minor: one step apart ("probable" vs "consistent with").
- no error: both findings use the same hedging level, or both are unhedged. A hedge on what the finding is counts: gt "haze, possibly pulmonary edema" is a possible edema, so a definite edema is two steps away. Only a hedge on why it is there, or on what accentuates it, leaves the finding itself definite: "possibly related to", "likely due to", "probably secondary to", "likely from cardiac decompensation", "possibly accentuated by positioning", or a hedged cause word ("likely reactive", "possibly post-operative").

**`measurement`**: sizes (`measurements`, or written in the text), judged against the structure and context (finding text, `clinical_significance`, `indication`).
- major: a difference that crosses a decision threshold of the structure: lung nodule 6, 8 and 15 mm (Fleischner, Lung-RADS), lymph node short axis 10 mm (15 mm for a target node on oncologic follow-up), adrenal nodule 1 and 4 cm, simple adnexal cyst 3, 5 and 10 cm (1 cm after menopause; O-RADS), thoracic aorta 4, 5 and 5.5 cm, abdominal aorta 3 and 5.5 cm (5 cm in women), subdural or epidural hematoma 10 mm, midline shift 5 mm, spleen 13 cm, common bile duct 6 mm (10 mm after cholecystectomy), main pancreatic duct 3, 5 and 10 mm, ureteral stone 5 and 10 mm; a difference of more than 20 % of the gt value and at least 2 mm on a lesion followed by its size (nodule, mass, lymph node, aneurysm, indeterminate lesion: a mass 3.0 vs 4.0 cm); a gt size the pred leaves out on a lesion followed or staged by its size: a mass or tumour, a lymph node of 10 mm or more, a lung nodule at or above 6 mm, an aneurysm or dilated aorta, an indeterminate lesion of 1 cm or more, an adnexal cyst over 3 cm (pred "right upper lobe mass" vs gt "4.2 cm right upper lobe mass"; pred "adrenal nodule" vs gt "1.4 cm adrenal nodule"), unless the pred gives a size class or bound that covers it (places it on the same side of every threshold of its structure: "subcentimeter" covers a 9 mm lymph node, not a 7 mm lung nodule; "enlarged" covers a lymph node over 10 mm) or another pred of the group gives the size.
- minor: a gt size the pred leaves out on a lesion below every threshold of its structure (a 4 mm lung nodule, an 8 mm lymph node) or on a finding not followed by its size (a sub-centimetre lesion too small to characterize, an effusion described by its grade); a difference or a left-out size on a benign lesion not followed by its size (simple cyst, calcified granuloma, fibroid, hemangioma, lipoma); a smaller difference that stays on one side of every threshold.
- no error: the same value; a size only the pred gives; a size class or bound that covers the gt value ("tiny" or "millimetric" for a gt under 6 mm, "subcentimeter" under 10 mm, "< 5 mm" for 4 mm, "enlarged" for a lymph node over 10 mm).

**`comparison`**: only a new or growing change ("new", "increased", "enlarging", "worsening") that one of the two findings gives and the other does not: the pred adds it, or leaves out the gt's ("enlarging mass" vs "mass"); every other comparison difference is scored upstream.
- major: on a finding followed by its size or number: a nodule, mass, lymph node, aneurysm or dilated aorta, effusion, metastasis.
- no error: on any other finding (the upstream flag keeps it minor).

---

## 3. OUTPUT

Return only a JSON object with one key, `errors_per_pair`: one entry `{"pair_id": <int>, "errors": [...]}` for every input pair, each `pair_id` exactly once. A pair without errors has `"errors": []`; no pairs → `{"errors_per_pair": []}`. Every error has a `dimension` from §2, a short, non-empty `reasoning` (one clause: what differs, and why it does or does not change management) and a `severity` of `"major"` or `"minor"`, in that order: write the reasoning before deciding the severity. Other dimension or severity values are dropped downstream. No text outside the JSON, no markdown fences.

```json
{
  "errors_per_pair": [
    {"pair_id": 1, "errors": [
      {"dimension": "site", "reasoning": "nodule in the right lower vs right middle lobe; the follow-up targets another lobe", "severity": "major"}
    ]},
    {"pair_id": 2, "errors": []},
    {"pair_id": 3, "errors": [
      {"dimension": "severity", "reasoning": "mild-to-moderate vs moderate effusion; one step, same management", "severity": "minor"}
    ]}
  ]
}
```

Output only the JSON object now.
